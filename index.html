EcoCAR PCM development challenge · Sept to Oct 2026 · Two-person team

A 10-speed transmission controller in Simulink and Stateflow

PCM's entry challenge was to write the controller for a 10-speed automatic transmission. We were given the plant, a longitudinal vehicle model with an engine, torque converter and gearbox, plus a system specification. Our controller reads the gear lever, throttle, brake, vehicle speed and engine RPM, then decides the gear, the parking brake and the final throttle.

We wrote the requirements first, built the gear-selector logic as a Stateflow state machine, and verified it with automated tests linked to each requirement. We presented it to the PCM leads at the McMaster Automotive Resource Centre (MARC), and it earned me my spot on the team.

PParkGear 0, parking brake on. Only when |v| < 0.1 m/s.
RReverseGear −1. Only when v < 0.5 m/s.
NNeutralGear 0. Allowed at any speed.
DDriveGears 1 to 10. Only when v > −0.5 m/s.

Problem

Engine stalled in Reverse

Engaging a gear at idle loads the engine through the torque converter, and RPM fell from 700 to about 250. We added idle throttle control at low RPM and tuned its threshold from 700 up to 1000 RPM.

Problem · found by me

Feedback wired to the wrong inputs

Speed, RPM and power were feeding the wrong controller ports. Our controller-only tests skipped the top-level wiring, so they never caught it. Lesson: test the whole system, not just the unit.

Problem · fixed by me

Tests that couldn't fail

Some test assessments had their timing backwards or no tolerance. I corrected them, added a 10–20 ms timing buffer, and cleaned up the requirement IDs and wording.

My part: reviewed the model integration and found the swapped feedback wiring, fixed the test assessments and requirement wording, and prepared our presentation.

MECHTRON 2TA4 · Embedded Systems Design I · Winter 2026

Nyan Cat Runner: an obstacle-avoidance game on a 16×2 LCD

A side-scrolling runner on a character LCD. The cat dodges star obstacles that scroll in from the right, faster and faster. One pushbutton makes it jump, the game keeps your score and best score, and a piezo buzzer beeps faster as the speed climbs and plays a crash sound when you hit a star.

The main loop runs on a fixed 20 Hz tick. A fractional speed accumulator lets obstacles move less than one column per tick, so scrolling stays smooth even though the LCD can only draw whole characters. The sprites are custom 5×8 pixel characters.

ENGINEER 1P13 · Integrated Cornerstone Design · Oct to Dec 2024

Automated airport luggage system

The brief: check passenger luggage in by ticket type (economy, business, layover) and move it from the conveyor platform down to the plane, using either a rotary or a linear actuator but not both. Our design catches the bag in a bucket, and a rotary actuator, geared up for torque, turns the bucket over so the bag slides onto the lower platform.

EcoCAR CAV development challenge · Sept 2026 · Pair project

AntWorld: an energy-aware colony controller

In C++, we wrote one controller that picks every ant's move each step. The colony's food map is built only from what the ants actually sense, and every trip is planned on energy cost. Unseen terrain is assumed to cost the maximum, so the colony never commits to a trip it can't afford.

Each ant's decision, in priority order
  1. Scan every step, since scanning costs no energy.
  2. Deliver: an ant carrying food heads home. If it runs out on the way, the food drops closer to home for another ant to finish.
  3. Collect: otherwise, go to the known food with the cheapest full trip, ant to food to home.
  4. Last trip: if no full trip is affordable, carry the cheapest reachable food closer to home.
  5. Explore: if no food is known, head to the first unexplored cell.

Modular guided cutting board

An assistive kitchen tool for a client with Usher syndrome, which limits her sight and hearing. The design had to be easy to use, high-contrast, easy to move around, low-cost and safe.

Attachable picket-style walls guide each cut, a roofed finger guard with stabilizing spikes protects her hand, and a custom knife handle keeps her grip steady and directed.

  • Team coordinator: logged every design studio and lab
  • Designed and modelled the knife handle in CAD; prototyped with 3D printing
  • Wrote the executive summary, problem framing and conceptual design sections, plus the testing plans

What testing taught us: using the board blindfolded showed how hard it was to use, so we redesigned the walls with slanted tops.

First-year design project · Jan to Feb 2025 · Subject matter expert

Wastewater filter material selection

We chose a filter material for algae-choked treatment ponds in Tampakan, Philippines, targeting contaminants of 1 to 100 µm. We ranked candidates in Ansys Granta EduPack using material performance indices for carbon footprint and production energy, and selected UHMWPE fibre.

We estimated porosity by comparing the Young's modulus of porous and solid material from Ashby charts and the Granta database. At about 7.3%, the fibre keeps the strength it needs for filtration.
